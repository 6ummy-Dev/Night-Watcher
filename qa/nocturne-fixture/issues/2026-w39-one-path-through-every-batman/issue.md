## One path through every Batman

Night Watcher is a map of every Batman story on screen: films and series, animated and live action, from the 1943 serial to titles dated for 2028. It is one page, kept by one fan, and it lives at [nightwatcher.life](https://nightwatcher.life/). You choose a path once. The page remembers where you stand, and it tells you what comes next.

The count, as it stands tonight, is 137 films, 71 seasons and 44 continuities. It moves when the studios move. Titles that are announced but not out sit on the shelf as parked. You can see them, and you cannot tick them until they land.

Every entry carries a year, a format, a tier and a where-to-watch search. The tier answers one question: should I watch this. Essentials are the spine, the core route is the long way round, and everything else is marked optional, as exactly that.

## Three ways to walk it

There are three orders, and the [README](https://github.com/6ummy-Dev/Night-Watcher/blob/main/README.md) names them. By universe keeps every continuity whole, each in its own story order. Bruce's life lines the stories up along one man's years. The app calls that an interpretation, not a canon, because it is one. Release order is the plain calendar, oldest first.

Pick one and the rest of the map follows it. Change your mind later and nothing you have ticked moves.

The Belt at the top narrows the view without changing the order: animated, live action or both; films alone or films and series; essentials, the core route, or everything. It says what it holds when it is closed, so you always know what you are looking at.

## Nothing ahead of what it gives away

The rule the whole map is built on is short. Nothing renders ahead of an entry it would give away. Universes stay whole, the blurbs describe a premise and never a turn, and the orders are checked so that a later story cannot sit in front of the one it spoils.

The [README](https://github.com/6ummy-Dev/Night-Watcher/blob/main/README.md) writes the rule down, and the build refuses a tree that breaks it. The paper keeps the same rule. If a piece of news gives away an ending, it stays in the drawer.

## What it will not do

There is no account and no sign-in. There are no ads, and nothing tracks what you watch. What you tick stays in your browser. It works offline once it has loaded, and the code is free software under the [AGPL](https://github.com/6ummy-Dev/Night-Watcher/blob/main/LICENSE).

That also means we do not know how many of you there are. We never will, and that is the point. The only number this page keeps is yours, and it keeps it on your side of the glass. If you want it somewhere else, the page hands you a backup code or a file, and you decide where it goes.

## Why a paper

The map changes when the studios announce, date or delay something. Until now those changes arrived quietly, one line at a time in the [changelog](https://github.com/6ummy-Dev/Night-Watcher/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md). Nocturne says them out loud, once a week, with the source beside every line.

Every Sunday, late, the Night Final covers the week's Batman news. Each story says where we read it and what it does to your watch order, and none of them spoil anything. Rumours do not run. A title with no date stays parked, and we say so. When a date moves, the map moves with it, and the paper tells you which way.

No. 0 is this one. No. 1 is next Sunday.
